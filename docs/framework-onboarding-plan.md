## 3. Research: how each family defines scoping and onboarding in practice

Method: five research agents (web), each reading primary sources; their reports are data. I spot-checked four consequential claims myself (marked "checked by me"). Confidence tags: V = read in the issuing body's own site/document; S = reputable secondary (certification body, audit firm, law firm, mirror of legal text); U = memory, snippet or paywalled. ### 3.1 ISO management systems (27001, 22301, 42001, 45001) -> `iso_management`
- 27001:2022 cl. 4.1-4.2 issues and interested parties WITH their requirements; 4.3 scope: boundaries, activities, products, locations, interfaces and dependencies, documented; exclusions justified; 5.3 roles assigned and communicated (no ISMS manager title is mandated: S); 6.1.2/6.1.3 risk criteria, treatment, Statement of Applicability (the auditor's central document). S: certification-body gap guides (NQA), Schellman, CSA, Advisera, DataGuard. ISO texts are paywalled; none read directly (iso.org/IAF/UKAS returned 403).
- What a certification body asks for before Stage 1 (quote forms): scope sentence (printed on the certificate), headcount in scope incl. part-time, shifts and staff per shift, permanent/temporary sites with activities, org chart, outsourced processes, technology sizing (systems, servers, networks, privileged users, remote users), legal/regulatory requirements, consultants used, prior certifications/transfer, three complexity categories 1-3 (business type, process type, management-system maturity). S (NSAI RFQ form; authoritative for NSAI only; bodies differ). ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024 counts freelancers and non-members as people for audit time (S, usd.de).
- Path: quote -> Stage 1 (documentation, readiness; checks internal audit and management review are planned/performed) -> Stage 2 -> nonconformity actions about 6 weeks -> decision -> 3-year certificate, annual surveillance (first within 12 months at TUV NORD; others differ), recertification before expiry. S (TUV NORD service description, NSAI). Transition: 2022 edition mandatory for new/recert audits from 30 Apr 2024; all transitions ended 31 Oct 2025 (V: INAB). Annex A 93 controls (S).
- NOT verified: a mandatory minimum operating period before Stage 2 ("3 months of records" is consultancy lore: U); the 2024 climate-change amendment (U: snippets; field is tagged unverified).
- 22301:2019 cl. 4.3: scope = products/services, parts of the organisation, locations; exclusions documented; BIA inputs (impact criteria, time frames, prioritised activities); supplier continuity (S: NQA checklist and gap guide). 42001:2023: role of the organisation (AI producer/provider/user from ISO/IEC 22989: S), AI system inventory incl. embedded third-party AI, impact assessment process 6.1.4, SoA, certification to ISO/IEC 17021-1 + 42006:2025 (S: Schellman, TCSA, ISOQAR). 45001:2018: workers and others under control, legal requirements, hazards, worker consultation, contractors (S: NSAI, LRQA, Munich Re).
- Sources actually read by the agent: tuev-nord service description PDF; nsai.ie RFQ docx + transition policy; inab.ie transition PDF; nqa.com gap guide, 22301 checklist and transition guide; dqsglobal; schellman scoping/timeline/42001 roles; cloudsecurityalliance.org ISO scope article; advisera clause 4 + SoA; dataguard 4.3; usd.de 27006-1; tcsa.in 42001; isoqar 42001 gap analysis; lrqa 45001 process; nsai 45001 deck; munichre/hsb.

### 3.2 Attestation and assurance (SOC 2, SOX ITGC, HITRUST, CSA STAR) -> `attestation`
- SOC 2 (V for category and type names: aicpa-cima.com; S for the rest): Security always; Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy optional, with the customer need recorded; Type I (design at a date) vs Type II (operation over a period); AICPA sets no minimum period (Schellman: AICPA guidance suggests at least 6 months, a 3-month first report is possible with disclosure; Linford: 12 typical; A-LIGN 3-12); management's system description (DC 200) with services, principal service commitments and system requirements, components (infrastructure, software, people, procedures, data) and boundaries, subservice organisations (carve-out vs inclusive), complementary user-entity controls; management assertion dated like the report; readiness -> dry run -> window. The DC 200 PDF needs an AICPA account and was NOT read (U for its exact wording). 2027 TSC exposure drafts were posted 26 Oct 2026 (V); the 2017/2022 criteria stay current until approved.
- SOX ITGC (V: SEC 33-8810, 15 USC 7262/7241, SEC filer definitions): management assessment of ICFR (404(a)), auditor attestation (404(b)) for accelerated and large accelerated filers (public float >= $75M / >= $700M), COSO 2013 named as a suitable framework; top-down scoping from significant accounts and relevant assertions to applications; IT scope = what the key controls rely on plus databases/OS/tools (S: KPMG, EY); service organisations via SOC 1 with bridge letters and CUECs. For Pakistan/Gulf banks SOX applies only through a US-listed entity: the field offers "Not an SEC issuer (voluntary ICFR)".
- HITRUST (V: Assessment Handbook v1.2): validated e1 (43 requirements, 1 year), i1 (182, 1 year), r2 (tailored, 2 years; interim in the last 90 days before the first anniversary); scope webforms (platforms/systems, facilities, outsourced services), r2 factors (organisational, geographic, system/technical, regulatory); systems must be in production at least 90 days; facilities hosting in-scope components must be in scope, homes cannot; carve-out of service providers allowed for e1/i1 not r2; assessor is a separate firm, staff who implemented controls in the last 12 months are excluded; fieldwork window at most 90 days.
- CSA STAR (V: cloudsecurityalliance.org): Level 1 self-assessment CAIQ (prerequisite), Level 2 attestation (SOC 2 + CCM) or certification (ISO 27001 + CCM); scope is the cloud offering, not the company; shared-responsibility per control; CCM v4.1 released 28 Jan 2026, v4.0 and v4.1 accepted until Dec 2027 (V).

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Concepts
- **Organisation profile**: ONE per tenant, asked once, edited under Compliance. Four groups = four stages: Entities and sites / Regulators and obligations / Data and systems / Risk and people (user's decision). 48 fields (`org_profile.json`); lists are tables whose rows get stable server-generated ids so a framework scope can pick rows by id; scalar organisation facts that already exist (`legal_entity`, `industry`, `company_size`, `geography`, `regulatory_scope`) are `maps_to: tenant.<column>` and written through to the Tenant row (single source of truth, audit-logged) - flagged D2. Units come from the department tree; key systems from the asset library; third parties may link a TPRM vendor.
- **Framework onboarding**: one per tenant per library framework. States: `not_started` (no row) -> `draft` -> `ready` (all required checks pass; waiting for approval) -> `complete` (approved, snapshot frozen) ; plus `underway` (the escape). Steps come from the catalog family of the framework; the engine adds a last stage "Review and approve".
- **Catalog = data**: `backend/grc/seed_data/framework_onboarding/*.json`, one file per family + `org_profile.json`. A framework is data, not code: slug -> family; steps -> fields; `applies_to` (slug list) narrows a step/field/gate check inside a family; `when` shows/requires a field conditionally; `level` required|advised|optional; every field has a one-sentence note ("why it is asked, who asks") and a source (its own, or its step's): ref, asked_by, confidence verified|secondary|unverified, urls. Labels are 1-3 words (text diet D41).
- **Gate** = (a) organisation profile complete for what the family needs (`requires_org` + the org profile's required fields and its "or none" checks), (b) every required field of every applicable step filled, (c) the family's explicit gate checks, (d) a recorded approval. Advised items show as advice and never block.

### 4.2 Field types (closed set)
text, longtext, number, date, bool, choice, multichoice, person (a user, or an external {name,email,organisation}), country, countries, table (rows with typed columns), asset_refs, vendor_ref, org_pick (rows picked from the organisation profile: `of` = entities|sites|systems|third_parties|data_types|departments), departments_derived (read-only, from the department tree). Table "required" means at least `min_rows` rows (default 1).
Gate check types: filled, true, equals, not_equals, in, includes, count_at_least, before (date a before date b), span_at_least_days, distinct (people differ), any_filled, table_rule (a column value forces another). `when`: {field, eq|ne|in|truthy}. Field references: `step.field` inside a family, `org.group.field` for the profile.

### 4.3 The written catalog so far (see the JSON for every note and source)
- Org profile, group by group (key: type, level): **entities_sites**: legal_entity text R (tenant), entity_kind choice A, industry text R (tenant), company_size text A (tenant), geography text A (tenant), headcount number R, annual_revenue number A, revenue_currency choice A, legal_entities table R (name, country, registration no., kind, headcount, regulated), sites table R (name, kind, country, city, headcount, shifts, tenure), units departments_derived A. **regulators_obligations**: jurisdictions countries R, regulatory_scope text O (tenant), regulators table R (+ no_regulator bool), certifications table R (+ no_certifications), audit_history table R (kind, subject, auditor, date, result, open findings, report ref; + no_prior_audits), external_requirements table A (required by regulator/customer/acquirer/parent/own decision, framework, deadline, mandatory). **data_systems**: classification_scheme text R, data_types table R (type, class, volume, stored in, systems, shared), special_environments multichoice R (Payment card processing, SWIFT connection, Industrial control or OT, AI systems built or used, Patient health information, US government CUI, Hosts customers' systems, Designated critical national infrastructure, None of these), hosting multichoice R, key_systems asset_refs R, asset_inventory choice R, third_parties table R (+ no_third_parties), data_flows choice R, data_flow_ref text O, data_subject_regions countries A. **risk_people**: risk_appetite choice R (Averse, Minimal, Cautious, Open, Hungry, Not defined), appetite_approval A, appetite_note O, risk_method choice R, risk_register A, security_policy A, cyber_reporting_line choice R, cyber_committee text A, people: sponsor R, compliance_lead R, ciso A, dpo A, cio A, head_of_risk A, internal_audit A, legal_counsel O, other_contacts table O. Cross-field checks: regulators / certifications / audits / third parties each "listed or none declared" (required), units defined, sponsor != compliance lead, appetite defined (advised).
- Family steps written (JSON in the repo): **iso_management** route, context, scope, method, roles, audit readiness; **attestation** engagement, system boundary, service providers, readiness; **payments** (PCI) level and route, card data scope, plan and dates, (SWIFT) architecture, attestation; **control_frameworks** purpose and scope, profile basis (CSF / 800-53 / 800-171 / AI RMF / CIS / COBIT variants through `applies_to`), method and dates.

## 6. The "already underway" path
- **Who may mark it**: a user with `compliance:framework_onboarding:override` (Administrator passes). **Reason**: required, at least 20 characters, plus a kind (Running before onboarding existed / Certified or attested outside the platform / Regulator-mandated start / Other), recorded with user and time in `grc_framework_onboarding_events`, shown on the framework page ("Underway since ... because ...").
- **What it does**: state `underway`; the journey may start; onboarding stays editable and can be completed later (then state `complete`). It does not hide the gaps: the readiness panel still lists what is missing.
- **Legacy**: a one-off, additive, idempotent backfill in `modules/compliance/schema_migrations.py` (`_ensure_for_engine`, same place as `_backfill_regulatory_task_twins`): for each `CertificationJourney` without an onboarding row insert one with `state=underway`, `source=legacy`, reason "A journey existed before framework onboarding". Today that is 4 rows on `1link`, 2 on `cfsb`, 0 on `complyverse`. The gate ALSO allows any framework that already has a journey (belt and braces, because those migrations swallow errors into the log). Nothing running is ever blocked, whatever the catalog later demands.
- Reopening a completed onboarding (reason required) never stops a running journey; it records an amendment (scope changes after sign-off are amendments, like TIBER-style scope control in the Cyber Security plan).

## 10. Decisions taken by default (the user may overrule)
D1 Ten families as in section 2 (NCA/PDPL catalog-only). D2 Five organisation facts are written through to Tenant columns. D3 Approval needs its own permission; four-eyes off. D4 Every existing journey becomes `underway/legacy` and is never blocked. D5 The gate is at journey creation (both creators); adding a framework to Controls Automation scope is not blocked, only its journey. D6 The profile needs about 20 required fields + 4 "listed or none" answers; the rest is advice. D7 Labels <= 3 words, explanations as (i) notes. D8 Reopening never stops a running journey. D9 The catalog is shipped data, versioned, not editable by tenants in v1; a snapshot keeps the version an approval used. D10 Data protection laws that are not in the library today (NCA, PDPL) are catalog-only. D11 Where the library's file disagrees with the current source (Aramco SACS-002 vs SACS-210; SBP "Framework for Digital Channels" not found; MAS Notices 644/655 cancelled) the onboarding follows the current source and says so. D12 Sources shown to users as small chips (reference + who asks + confidence), URLs behind a details control (U1).
