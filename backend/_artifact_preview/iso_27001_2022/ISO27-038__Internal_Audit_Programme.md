## Purpose & Objectives

This Internal Audit Programme defines the multi-year plan for conducting internal audits of the organisation’s Information Security Management System (ISMS) in accordance with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.2.

The purpose of the programme is to provide a structured, risk-based, and independent approach for evaluating whether the ISMS:

- Conforms to the organisation’s own information security requirements, policies, processes, and documented ISMS arrangements.
- Conforms to the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
- Is effectively implemented, maintained, and continually improved.
- Adequately addresses information security risks, objectives, and applicable controls.
- Provides reliable audit results for management review, corrective action, certification readiness, and continual improvement.

The objectives of this programme are to:

1. Establish a planned audit schedule covering the full ISO/IEC 27001:2022 ISMS over a defined multi-year cycle.
2. Ensure all applicable ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses, Annex A controls, ISMS processes, in-scope locations, business functions, and supporting services are audited at appropriate intervals.
3. Prioritise audits based on information security risk, business criticality, previous audit results, changes, incidents, regulatory requirements, and management priorities.
4. Ensure internal audits are performed objectively and impartially by competent auditors who do not audit their own work.
5. Define milestones, resources, roles, reporting expectations, and success measures for the audit programme.
6. Support timely identification, recording, communication, correction, and verification of nonconformities and improvement opportunities.
7. Provide documented evidence that the organisation has implemented and maintained an effective internal audit programme as required by ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.2.

## Scope

This plan applies to internal audits of the ISMS within the defined ISMS scope, including the organisational units, locations, processes, technologies, services, people, and third-party interfaces that support the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets.

The audit programme covers:

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clauses 4 to 10, including:
  - Context of the organisation.
  - Leadership and information security governance.
  - Planning, including information security risk assessment and risk treatment.
  - Support, including competence, awareness, communication, and documented information.
  - Operation of ISMS processes.
  - Performance evaluation, including monitoring, measurement, internal audit, and management review.
  - Improvement, including nonconformity and corrective action.
- Applicable Annex A controls from ISO/IEC 27001:2022 as selected in the Statement of Applicability.
- ISMS policies, standards, procedures, guidelines, and operating practices.
- Risk assessment, risk treatment, control implementation, control ownership, and residual risk acceptance activities.
- Security monitoring, incident management, access control, supplier security, business continuity, asset management, secure configuration, vulnerability management, and other control domains relevant to the ISMS.
- Interfaces with related governance, risk, compliance, privacy, technology, resilience, and supplier management processes.
- Follow-up audits to verify closure and effectiveness of corrective actions.

The programme does not replace:

- External certification or surveillance audits.
- Customer, regulator, or supplier audits.
- Technical security testing such as penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, or configuration assessments, although results from those activities may inform audit planning.
- Operational quality assurance reviews, unless they are explicitly included as ISMS audit evidence.

## Approach / Phases

The Internal Audit Programme follows a risk-based, cyclical approach aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.2. The programme is designed to ensure that audit criteria and scope are established for each audit, auditors are selected to ensure objectivity and impartiality, results are reported to relevant management, and documented information is retained as evidence of programme implementation and audit results.

### Phase 1: Programme Planning

The ISMS Manager, in coordination with the Internal Audit Lead and relevant leadership stakeholders, shall establish or refresh the audit programme at least annually.

Planning inputs include:

- ISMS scope and any planned scope changes.
- Business and technology changes affecting information security.
- Results of information security risk assessments.
- Risk treatment priorities.
- Statement of Applicability and control applicability decisions.
- Previous audit findings, nonconformities, and corrective action status.
- Results from certification, customer, supplier, regulatory, and assurance reviews.
- Security incident trends and major events.
- Monitoring and measurement results.
- Management review outputs.
- Resource availability and auditor competence.
- Criticality of processes, systems, data, suppliers, and locations.

Audit frequency shall be proportionate to risk. High-risk or high-change areas may be audited annually or more frequently. Lower-risk, stable areas may be audited within the multi-year cycle, provided all ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements and applicable controls are covered within the defined programme period.

### Phase 2: Audit Scheduling and Coverage Mapping

The annual schedule shall be derived from the multi-year audit programme and shall identify:

- Audit title or theme.
- Relevant ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses.
- Relevant Annex A control domains or control references.
- In-scope organisational units, locations, systems, suppliers, or processes.
- Planned timing.
- Lead auditor and supporting auditors.
- Auditees and process owners.
- Audit method, such as interview, document review, sampling, walkthrough, observation, or evidence testing.
- Expected outputs.

Audit coverage shall be mapped to ensure that:

- All ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses applicable to the ISMS are audited over the programme cycle.
- All applicable Annex A controls are audited based on risk and applicability.
- Critical ISMS processes are audited at least once per year where warranted by risk.
- Follow-up audits are scheduled where significant or repeated nonconformities exist.
- Audit timing supports management review and external certification or surveillance audit readiness.

### Phase 3: Individual Audit Planning

For each scheduled audit, the assigned Lead Auditor shall prepare an audit plan before fieldwork begins. The plan shall define:

- Audit objective.
- Audit criteria.
- Audit scope and boundaries.
- Audit dates and locations.
- Auditees and interviewees.
- Audit team members.
- Evidence to be reviewed.
- Sampling approach.
- Confidentiality requirements.
- Opening and closing meeting arrangements.
- Reporting timeline.

Audit criteria may include:

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements.
- Statement of Applicability.
- Approved ISMS policies and procedures.
- Risk treatment plans.
- Legal, regulatory, contractual, and customer requirements.
- Applicable internal standards and control baselines.

### Phase 4: Audit Execution

Audits shall be executed using evidence-based methods. The audit team shall collect and evaluate objective evidence through:

- Interviews with personnel.
- Review of documented information.
- Sampling of records and transactions.
- Walkthroughs of processes.
- Observation of activities and control operation.
- Review of system-generated reports and security tooling outputs.
- Traceability testing from requirement to implementation to evidence.

Audit findings shall be based on verifiable evidence and categorised consistently. Typical categories include:

| Finding Category | Description | Expected Action |
|---|---|---|
| Major Nonconformity | Absence, systemic failure, or significant breakdown of an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirement or ISMS control that may affect the ISMS’s ability to achieve intended outcomes. | Immediate containment, root cause analysis, corrective action plan, management escalation, and effectiveness verification. |
| Minor Nonconformity | Isolated failure to meet a requirement where the overall process remains generally effective. | Corrective action with assigned owner and due date. |
| Opportunity for Improvement | A condition that does not constitute nonconformity but may improve efficiency, effectiveness, resilience, or auditability. | Management evaluation and optional improvement action. |

Audit conclusions shall distinguish between facts, evidence, risks, and recommendations. Auditors shall avoid consulting on or designing controls for areas they audit in a way that compromises independence.

### Phase 5: Reporting and Communication

The Lead Auditor shall prepare an audit report after each audit. Reports shall be provided to relevant management and the ISMS Manager within the agreed reporting timeframe.

Each audit report should include:

- Audit objective, scope, and criteria.
- Audit dates and audit team.
- Auditees and functions reviewed.
- Summary conclusion.
- Areas of conformity.
- Nonconformities and supporting evidence.
- Opportunities for improvement.
- Constraints or limitations encountered.
- Required corrective actions.
- Agreed owners and target dates.
- Follow-up requirements.

Material audit results, significant nonconformities, overdue corrective actions, and recurring themes shall be reported to management review in accordance with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.3.

### Phase 6: Corrective Action and Follow-Up

Nonconformities identified through internal audits shall be managed through the ISMS corrective action process.

For each nonconformity, the responsible owner shall:

1. Assess the significance and potential impact.
2. Take containment action where appropriate.
3. Determine root cause.
4. Define corrective action to eliminate the cause.
5. Assign an accountable owner.
6. Establish a target completion date.
7. Implement corrective action.
8. Provide evidence of completion.
9. Support effectiveness verification.

The auditor or an independent reviewer shall verify whether corrective actions have been implemented and are effective. Verification may include evidence review, re-testing, interviews, or follow-up audit activity.

### Phase 7: Programme Monitoring and Improvement

The ISMS Manager and Internal Audit Lead shall monitor the performance of the audit programme. Programme performance shall be reviewed at least annually and as an input to management review.

The programme shall be updated where results indicate:

- Insufficient audit coverage.
- Repeated or systemic findings.
- Changes in information security risk.
- Significant organisational or technology changes.
- Auditor resource or competence gaps.
- Delayed or ineffective corrective action closure.
- Need for greater alignment with certification, customer, or regulatory expectations.

## Roles & Responsibilities

The following roles are responsible for planning, executing, supporting, reporting, and improving the internal audit programme.

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Top Management | Approves the audit programme direction where required; ensures audit independence and availability of resources; reviews significant audit results; supports timely corrective action; considers audit outputs during management review. |
| ISMS Manager | Owns this Internal Audit Programme; ensures alignment with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.2; coordinates annual audit planning; maintains audit records; tracks audit coverage; reports audit performance to management review. |
| Internal Audit Lead | Develops the detailed audit schedule; assigns auditors; ensures audit plans are prepared; confirms auditor competence and independence; reviews audit reports for quality and consistency. |
| Lead Auditor | Plans and conducts assigned audits; defines audit objectives, scope, and criteria; leads opening and closing meetings; collects objective evidence; prepares audit reports; identifies nonconformities and opportunities for improvement. |
| Supporting Auditor / Subject Matter Auditor | Supports evidence collection and analysis; contributes technical expertise; documents audit observations; follows audit methodology and confidentiality requirements. |
| Process Owner / Control Owner | Participates in audits; provides access to records, personnel, and systems; validates factual accuracy of findings; owns corrective actions assigned to their area. |

| Corrective Action Owner | Performs root cause analysis; defines and implements corrective action; provides closure evidence; supports effectiveness verification. |

## Review & Update Triggers

This Internal Audit Programme shall be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur that may affect audit priorities, scope, frequency, resources, or effectiveness.

The programme shall be updated when any of the following triggers occur:

- Change to ISMS scope.
- Change to ISO/IEC 27001 certification status, certification body expectations, or external audit schedule.
- Significant organisational restructuring, merger, acquisition, divestment, or outsourcing.
- Introduction of new critical systems, platforms, products, services, or locations within ISMS scope.
- Major change to information security risk profile.
- Significant security incident or near miss.
- Material legal, regulatory, contractual, or customer requirement change.
- Significant supplier, cloud service, or outsourced service change.
- Repeated, systemic, or major nonconformities.
- Corrective actions are overdue, ineffective, or recurring.
- Changes to the Statement of Applicability or risk treatment plan.
- Management review identifies need for revised audit focus.
- External audit results indicate gaps in internal audit coverage or effectiveness.
- Auditor resource, competence, or independence constraints arise.
- Changes to ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27002, or relevant implementation guidance affecting the ISMS.

Review records shall include:

- Date of review.
- Reviewer name and role.
- Inputs considered.
- Changes made to the audit programme.
- Rationale for changes.
- Approval, where required.
- Communication to relevant stakeholders.
