## Purpose

This Policy establishes mandatory requirements for identifying, analysing, documenting, monitoring, and reviewing the internal and external issues that are relevant to the organisation’s purpose and that affect its ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the Information Security Management System (ISMS).

This Policy supports ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 4.1 by ensuring that the organisation maintains a current and evidence-based understanding of its operating context, including business, legal, regulatory, technological, threat, cultural, economic, and organisational factors that may influence information security risks, opportunities, and ISMS planning.

## Scope

This Policy applies to:

- The ISMS and all organisational units, business functions, locations, processes, personnel, and third parties within the approved ISMS scope.
- All activities related to establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving the ISMS.
- Internal and external issues that may affect:
  - The confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets.
  - The organisation’s ability to meet information security objectives.
  - The design and operation of ISMS processes.
  - The identification and treatment of information security risks and opportunities.
  - Compliance with applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and business requirements.

This Policy applies to all employees, contractors, suppliers, service providers, and other relevant parties who contribute to or influence ISMS planning, operation, governance, or assurance activities.

## Policy Statements

1. The organisation shall determine the internal and external issues that are relevant to its purpose and that affect its ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the ISMS.

2. The organisation shall document relevant internal and external issues in an approved ISMS Context Register, strategy document, risk management tool, governance repository, or equivalent controlled record.

3. The organisation shall ensure that identified context issues are sufficiently specific to support ISMS planning, information security risk assessment, risk treatment, control selection, and continual improvement.

4. The organisation shall consider, at a minimum, the following categories of external issues when determining the ISMS context:
   - Legal, statutory, regulatory, and contractual obligations.
   - Industry, market, customer, and stakeholder expectations.
   - Cyber threat landscape and emerging attack methods.
   - Technology trends, cloud adoption, outsourcing, and supply chain dependencies.
   - Geopolitical, economic, environmental, and social factors.
   - Requirements or guidance from regulators, certification bodies, insurers, and industry associations.

5. The organisation shall consider, at a minimum, the following categories of internal issues when determining the ISMS context:
   - Organisational strategy, mission, objectives, and business model.
   - Governance structure, decision-making processes, and accountability arrangements.
   - Information assets, business processes, and operational dependencies.
   - Existing security capabilities, control maturity, and known weaknesses.
   - Organisational culture, personnel capability, resourcing, and awareness.
   - Technology architecture, legacy systems, integrations, and data flows.
   - Prior incidents, audit results, risk assessments, management reviews, and lessons learned.

6. The organisation shall evaluate each identified issue for its relevance to the ISMS and its potential impact on information security risks, opportunities, objectives, controls, and resources.

7. The organisation shall maintain traceability between significant context issues and relevant ISMS outputs, including the ISMS scope, information security risk assessment, risk treatment plan, Statement of Applicability, information security objectives, policies, and management review inputs.

8. The organisation shall review context issues when significant changes occur, including but not limited to:
   - New or changed laws, regulations, contractual obligations, or customer requirements.
   - Material changes to business strategy, products, services, or operating model.
   - Mergers, acquisitions, divestitures, restructuring, or major organisational change.
   - Introduction of new technologies, platforms, outsourced services, or suppliers.
   - Significant information security incidents, vulnerabilities, threats, or audit findings.
   - Changes in the geopolitical, economic, environmental, or industry landscape.

9. The organisation shall assign ownership for monitoring relevant context areas to appropriate business, technology, risk, legal, compliance, procurement, human resources, and information security stakeholders.

10. The organisation shall ensure that context analysis is performed using reliable and relevant information sources, such as risk assessments, threat intelligence, legal and regulatory updates, business plans, audit results, customer requirements, supplier assessments, incident trends, and management review outputs.

11. The organisation shall ensure that material changes in internal or external context are communicated to relevant ISMS stakeholders in a timely manner.

12. The organisation shall use the results of context analysis to inform information security governance decisions, including resource allocation, control prioritisation, risk acceptance, risk treatment, policy updates, and continual improvement actions.

13. The organisation shall retain documented information as evidence that internal and external issues have been determined, monitored, reviewed, and considered in ISMS planning and improvement activities.

14. The organisation shall ensure that context-related documented information is protected from unauthorised modification, deletion, or disclosure in accordance with the organisation’s information classification and document control requirements.

15. The organisation shall include significant context changes and their ISMS implications as inputs to management review.

16. The organisation shall ensure that personnel involved in determining and reviewing context issues have sufficient competence, authority, and access to relevant information to perform their responsibilities effectively.

17. The organisation shall not rely on generic or static assumptions when determining its ISMS context; context information shall reflect the organisation’s actual operating environment, obligations, dependencies, and risk exposure.

18. The organisation shall maintain alignment between the ISMS context and related ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements, including Clause 4.2 Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties, Clause 4.3 Determining the scope of the ISMS, Clause 6.1 Actions to address risks and opportunities, and Clause 9.3 Management review.

19. The organisation shall record decisions where identified context issues are determined not to be relevant to the ISMS, including the rationale for exclusion where such exclusion could reasonably be challenged during audit, assurance, or regulatory review.

20. The organisation shall ensure that context analysis supports continual improvement of the ISMS by identifying changes that may require updated controls, revised risk treatment, additional resources, or modified information security objectives.

The following table defines the minimum context categories that shall be assessed and maintained as documented information:

| Context Category | Issue Type | Minimum Considerations | Example Issue | Expected ISMS Linkage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regulatory and legal environment | External | Applicable information security, privacy, sector, and data protection obligations | New data protection reporting requirement applies to customer breach notifications | Compliance obligations, risk assessment, incident response procedures |
| Technology and infrastructure | Internal / External | Cloud services, SaaS platforms, legacy systems, integrations, architecture changes | Migration of customer data processing to [Cloud Service Provider] | ISMS scope, supplier risk, access control, encryption requirements |
| Threat landscape | External | Threat actors, attack trends, vulnerabilities, sector-specific targeting | Increased phishing and credential theft campaigns targeting the industry | Security awareness, email security controls, monitoring, risk treatment |
| Organisational strategy | Internal | Business objectives, growth plans, acquisitions, new products or services | Launch of [New Digital Service] involving sensitive customer data | ISMS scope, privacy/security by design, risk assessment |
| Supplier and outsourcing dependency | External | Critical suppliers, managed service providers, fourth parties, concentration risk | Dependence on [Managed Service Provider] for security monitoring | Supplier assurance, contractual controls, continuity planning |

## Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Board / Governing Body | Provides oversight of the ISMS context where it affects strategic risk, legal obligations, reputation, resilience, or business objectives. Ensures adequate resources are available to address material context issues. |
| Executive Management | Ensures internal and external issues relevant to the ISMS are considered in business planning, organisational change, major investments, and strategic decision-making. |
| ISMS Manager / CISO | Owns this Policy, coordinates context analysis, maintains documented information, ensures linkage to ISMS planning, and reports significant changes to management review. |
| Risk Management Function | Supports assessment of context issues for risk significance, ensures alignment with enterprise risk management, and validates risk-related implications. |
| Legal and Compliance Function | Monitors applicable legal, statutory, regulatory, and contractual changes and communicates relevant obligations affecting the ISMS. |
| Data Protection / Privacy Function | Identifies privacy, personal data, and data protection issues that may affect the ISMS context, risks, controls, and obligations. |
| IT and Security Operations | Identifies technology, infrastructure, operational, vulnerability, threat, and incident trends that may affect the ISMS. |
| Procurement / Vendor Management | Identifies supplier, outsourcing, third-party, and supply chain issues that may affect ISMS scope, risk, controls, or obligations. |
| Human Resources | Identifies workforce, culture, competence, insider risk, onboarding, offboarding, and organisational change issues relevant to information security. |
| Business Process Owners | Identify business process, product, service, customer, and operational issues that may affect information security risks or ISMS objectives. |
| Internal Audit | Provides independent assurance over the effectiveness of the process for determining, documenting, monitoring, and reviewing ISMS context. |
| All Personnel | Report relevant business, operational, technological, regulatory, supplier, or threat changes that may affect information security or ISMS effectiveness. |

## Compliance, Monitoring & Enforcement

Compliance with this Policy is mandatory.

The organisation shall monitor compliance with this Policy through appropriate governance, assurance, and review activities, including:

- Periodic review of the ISMS Context Register or equivalent documented information.
- Verification that context issues are considered during information security risk assessments.
- Review of management review inputs and outputs relating to changes in internal and external issues.
- Internal audits against ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 4.1 and related clauses.
- Review of legal, regulatory, contractual, supplier, technology, incident, and threat intelligence updates.
- Assessment of whether material context changes are reflected in the ISMS scope, risk treatment plan, Statement of Applicability, objectives, and controls.

The following compliance measures shall be applied:

| Monitoring Activity | Frequency | Responsible Role | Evidence Required |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Review of ISMS Context Register or equivalent documented record | At least annually and upon significant change | ISMS Manager / CISO | Updated context record, review notes, approvals |
| Legal and regulatory horizon scanning | At least quarterly | Legal and Compliance Function | Regulatory update log, obligation assessment, action records |
| Threat landscape and technology change review | At least quarterly | Security Operations / IT Leadership | Threat intelligence summary, vulnerability trends, architecture change records |
| Management review consideration of context changes | At planned management review intervals | Executive Management / ISMS Manager | Management review agenda, minutes, decisions, actions |
| Internal audit of Clause 4.1 implementation | As per internal audit programme | Internal Audit | Audit plan, audit report, findings, corrective actions |

Non-compliance with this Policy may result in corrective action, including:

- Mandatory remediation planning.
- Escalation to executive management or the risk governance forum.
- Revision of risk assessments, treatment plans, policies, procedures, or controls.
- Additional training or awareness activities.
- Disciplinary action in accordance with organisational policies where non-compliance is intentional, negligent, or repeated.
- Supplier contractual remedies where non-compliance involves third parties.

Failure to identify, communicate, or act upon material internal or external issues that affect the ISMS shall be treated as an ISMS nonconformity and managed through the organisation’s corrective action process.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to this Policy shall be permitted only where there is a documented and approved business justification.

All exception requests shall include:

- The specific Policy requirement for which an exception is requested.
- The reason the requirement cannot be met.
- The affected business area, process, system, supplier, or information asset.
- The risk assessment of granting the exception.
- Compensating controls or alternative arrangements.
- The proposed exception duration.
- The accountable exception owner.
- Approval from the ISMS Manager / CISO and relevant risk authority.

Exceptions shall not be approved where they would prevent the organisation from satisfying ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 4.1 or materially impair the organisation’s ability to maintain an effective ISMS.

Approved exceptions shall be recorded, tracked, periodically reviewed, and time-bound. Permanent exceptions are not permitted unless formally incorporated into an approved revision of this Policy or associated ISMS documentation.

| Exception Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Maximum approval period | [12 months] unless a shorter period is required by risk decision |
| Minimum approver | ISMS Manager / CISO and relevant business risk owner |
| Review frequency | At least quarterly or upon material change |
| Required evidence | Exception request, risk assessment, compensating controls, approval record |

## Review & Maintenance

This Policy shall be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur that may affect the ISMS context or the organisation’s ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the ISMS.

Triggers for review shall include, but are not limited to:

- Changes to ISO/IEC 27001, related standards, or certification requirements.
- Significant changes in laws, regulations, contractual obligations, or customer requirements.
- Major organisational restructuring, acquisition, divestiture, or strategic change.
- Introduction or retirement of significant technologies, platforms, suppliers, or services.
- Significant information security incidents, audit findings, risk events, or control failures.
- Changes to the ISMS scope, risk methodology, governance model, or information security objectives.
- Material changes in the threat landscape or external operating environment.

The ISMS Manager / CISO shall be responsible for coordinating review and maintenance of this Policy. Updates shall be approved in accordance with the organisation’s document control and ISMS governance requirements.

Documented evidence of Policy review shall be retained, including review dates, reviewers, changes made, approval records, and version history.
