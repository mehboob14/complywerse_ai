## Purpose & Objectives

This ISMS Project Charter formally authorises the implementation of an Information Security Management System (ISMS) aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022. It establishes the leadership mandate, project sponsorship, implementation scope, governance approach, resourcing, budget authority, timeline, and expected outcomes for the organisation’s ISO/IEC 27001:2022 implementation.

This Charter supports ISO/IEC 27001:2022 by demonstrating that top management provides leadership and commitment to the ISMS by:

- Ensuring the information security policy and information security objectives are established and compatible with the strategic direction of the organisation.
- Ensuring integration of ISMS requirements into organisational processes.
- Ensuring that resources needed for the ISMS are available.
- Communicating the importance of effective information security management and conformity with ISMS requirements.
- Ensuring the ISMS achieves its intended outcomes.
- Directing and supporting personnel to contribute to ISMS effectiveness.
- Promoting continual improvement.
- Supporting management roles relevant to information security.

The objectives of this project are to:

1. Establish, implement, maintain, and prepare for certification of an ISMS conforming to ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
2. Define and approve the ISMS scope, including organisational units, locations, technologies, information assets, business processes, and interfaces with interested parties.
3. Conduct a formal information security risk assessment and risk treatment process aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
4. Develop and approve the Statement of Applicability, including control applicability decisions for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls.
5. Implement required governance, risk management, operational, technical, and assurance activities necessary to meet ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements.
6. Establish measurable information security objectives and performance monitoring mechanisms.
7. Conduct internal audit and management review prior to external certification assessment.
8. Demonstrate visible executive sponsorship and sustained leadership commitment throughout ISMS implementation and operation.

The authorised sponsor for this initiative is [Executive Sponsor Role], acting on behalf of top management. The designated ISMS implementation owner is [ISMS Manager Role].

## Approach / Phases

The ISMS implementation will follow a structured project lifecycle designed to establish effective governance, implement ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements, generate objective evidence, and prepare the organisation for certification.

### Phase 1 — Initiation and Leadership Mandate

This phase establishes formal authority, sponsorship, governance, and project controls.

Key activities:

- Approve this ISMS Project Charter.
- Appoint the Executive Sponsor, ISMS Manager, risk owners, control owners, and project working group.
- Confirm top management expectations for the ISMS, including certification intent.
- Define project governance, escalation paths, reporting cadence, and decision forums.
- Confirm budget parameters and procurement requirements.
- Communicate project launch and leadership commitment to relevant personnel.

Expected outputs:

- Approved ISMS Project Charter.
- Project governance structure.
- Initial stakeholder register.
- Project communication plan.
- Confirmed sponsor and ISMS leadership roles.

### Phase 2 — Context, Scope, and Planning

This phase determines the organisational context and defines the boundaries of the ISMS.

Key activities:

- Identify internal and external issues relevant to information security.
- Identify interested parties and their information security requirements.
- Define and approve the ISMS scope.
- Identify applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and business requirements.
- Establish information security policy principles.
- Establish information security objectives aligned with strategic direction.
- Define documented information requirements.
- Confirm project plan, budget, and resource allocation.

Expected outputs:

- Context and interested parties analysis.
- Legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements register.
- Approved ISMS Scope Statement.
- Draft or approved Information Security Policy.
- Information security objectives.
- Document register and evidence plan.

### Phase 3 — Risk Assessment and Statement of Applicability

This phase establishes the risk-based foundation for the ISMS and determines applicable controls.

Key activities:

- Approve the information security risk assessment and risk treatment methodology.
- Identify in-scope assets, information types, processes, systems, and owners.
- Conduct risk assessments with business and technology stakeholders.
- Define risk acceptance criteria and assign risk ownership.
- Select risk treatment options.
- Map risk treatments to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls and other relevant controls.
- Develop the Statement of Applicability.
- Obtain management approval for residual risks and risk treatment plans.

Expected outputs:

- Risk assessment methodology.
- Asset and process inventory.
- Risk register.
- Risk treatment plan.
- Statement of Applicability.
- Approved risk acceptance records.

### Phase 4 — ISMS Design and Control Implementation

This phase implements required management system processes and selected controls.

Key activities:

- Develop or update ISMS policies, standards, procedures, plans, and records.
- Implement organisational controls, including access governance, asset ownership, supplier security, incident management, and change management alignment.
- Implement people controls, including screening, terms and conditions, security awareness, disciplinary process alignment, and role-based competence.
- Implement physical controls applicable to in-scope facilities and assets.
- Implement technological controls, including identity and access management, logging, vulnerability management, secure configuration, backup, endpoint protection, network security, encryption, and secure development where applicable.
- Establish operational monitoring and evidence collection.
- Conduct training and awareness activities.
- Track and report implementation progress and risks.

Expected outputs:

- Approved ISMS documentation set.
- Control implementation evidence.
- Training and awareness records.
- Supplier security records.
- Access review evidence.
- Vulnerability and remediation records.
- Incident response and business continuity integration records.
- Updated risk treatment plan.

### Phase 5 — Performance Evaluation and Internal Assurance

This phase evaluates ISMS effectiveness and readiness for external certification.

Key activities:

- Monitor and measure information security objectives and control performance.
- Conduct ISMS internal audit covering ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses and applicable Annex A controls.
- Record audit findings, nonconformities, observations, and improvement opportunities.
- Conduct management review with top management.
- Evaluate progress against intended ISMS outcomes.
- Confirm adequacy of resources and ongoing leadership commitment.
- Approve corrective action plans.

Expected outputs:

- Monitoring and measurement results.
- Internal audit plan and report.
- Corrective action register.
- Management review minutes and decisions.
- Updated risk register and treatment plan.
- Audit readiness assessment.

### Phase 6 — Certification Readiness and External Audit Support

This phase prepares the organisation for certification assessment and supports audit activities.

Key activities:

- Select and contract an accredited certification body.
- Prepare evidence repository and audit logistics.
- Conduct Stage 1 readiness review.
- Address Stage 1 findings or concerns.
- Conduct Stage 2 certification audit.
- Respond to audit findings and nonconformities.
- Submit corrective action evidence as required.
- Transition project governance into ongoing ISMS operational governance.

Expected outputs:

- Certification audit schedule.
- Stage 1 audit evidence and findings response.
- Stage 2 audit evidence and findings response.
- Certification decision or corrective action plan.
- ISMS operational governance handover.
- Continual improvement backlog.
