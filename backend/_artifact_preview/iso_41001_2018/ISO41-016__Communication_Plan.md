## Purpose & Objectives

The purpose of this Communication Plan is to define the planned communications necessary to support the effective operation, maintenance, improvement, and assurance of the organisation’s Facility Management System (FMS) in accordance with ISO 41001:2018.

This Plan establishes what facility management communications are required, when they occur, with whom they are conducted, how they are delivered, and who is responsible for communicating. It ensures that relevant internal and external stakeholders receive timely, accurate, consistent, and appropriate information relating to facility management services, performance, changes, risks, incidents, responsibilities, and continual improvement.

The objectives of this Plan are to:

- Ensure facility management communications are planned, controlled, and aligned with organisational objectives and stakeholder needs.
- Support effective delivery of FM services, including both in-house and outsourced activities.
- Enable users, clients, service providers, contractors, leadership, and other interested parties to understand relevant FM requirements, service expectations, responsibilities, and changes.
- Provide communication arrangements for normal operations, planned changes, emergency or disruptive events, service performance, compliance obligations, and management review inputs.
- Ensure communications are clear, timely, accessible, authorised, and retained where required as documented information.
- Support evidence-based assurance of ISO 41001:2018 during internal audits, certification audits, and management reviews.
- Promote engagement, awareness, coordination, and feedback across all parties involved in or affected by the FMS.

## Scope

This Communication Plan applies to all communications necessary for the effective implementation, operation, performance evaluation, and improvement of the FMS.

The scope includes communications related to:

- FM policy, objectives, roles, responsibilities, and authority.
- FM service delivery, service levels, operational updates, planned works, maintenance activities, and service disruptions.
- Applicable legal, regulatory, statutory, contractual, health, safety, environmental, security, accessibility, and business continuity requirements affecting FM services.
- Communication with internal users, client functions, senior management, employees, site occupants, visitors, contractors, outsourced service providers, landlords, tenants, regulators, emergency responders, and other relevant interested parties.
- Performance monitoring, service review, customer satisfaction, complaints, corrective actions, risks, opportunities, and continual improvement.
- Emergency, incident, crisis, and business disruption communications where these relate to facilities or FM services.
- Communication channels used for FM notifications, including meetings, email, intranet, service portals, helpdesk systems, signage, dashboards, contract governance forums, mobile alerts, and formal reports.
- Retention of FM communication records where evidence is required for service assurance, compliance, audit, contractual governance, or incident investigation.

This Plan applies to:

- All facilities, buildings, sites, workspaces, assets, and services included within the defined FMS scope.
- All FM personnel, whether permanent, temporary, contractor, consultant, or outsourced provider.
- All parties communicating on behalf of the FMS.

This Plan does not replace detailed incident response, emergency management, health and safety, business continuity, change management, or contractor management procedures. Where such procedures define specific communication steps, this Plan aligns with and references them as supporting arrangements.

## Approach / Phases

The organisation will implement and maintain FM communications through the following planned approach.

### Phase 1 — Define Communication Requirements

The FM System Owner, supported by FM governance, site management, compliance, and service delivery leads, shall identify communication requirements necessary to operate and improve the FMS.

Activities include:

- Review ISO 41001:2018 requirements and related FMS processes.
- Identify internal and external interested parties relevant to FM services.
- Determine the information each interested party needs to receive or provide.
- Identify communication obligations arising from:
 - FM policy and objectives.
 - Service level agreements and contracts.
 - Legal, regulatory, statutory, and compliance requirements.
 - Health, safety, environmental, security, accessibility, and business continuity requirements.
 - Incident, emergency, change, and maintenance processes.
 - Management review, audit, risk, corrective action, and continual improvement processes.
- Define mandatory communication attributes:
 - What will be communicated.
 - When communication will occur.
 - With whom communication will take place.
 - How communication will be delivered.
 - Who is responsible for communication.
 - What records will be retained.

### Phase 3 — Establish Communication Governance

To ensure consistency and control, the following governance principles apply:

- FM communications shall be proportionate to the risk, urgency, complexity, and stakeholder impact of the matter being communicated.
- Communications affecting safety, security, compliance, building access, service availability, contractual obligations, or reputation shall be authorised by the designated responsible role before release, except where immediate emergency communication is required.
- Messages shall be accurate, clear, concise, audience-appropriate, and action-oriented.
- Communications shall identify:
 - The issue or topic.
 - Who is affected.
 - Required action by recipients, if any.
 - Effective date, time, and duration.
 - Point of contact or escalation route.
 - Next update timing where ongoing.
- Communications shall be accessible to intended recipients, considering:
 - Physical location and work pattern.
 - Language needs.
 - Disability and accessibility needs.
 - Digital access limitations.
 - Contractor and visitor access to communication channels.
- Where FM services are outsourced, communication requirements shall be included in contracts, service level agreements, mobilisation plans, and governance meetings.
- Records of significant FM communications shall be retained as documented information where required for compliance, audit, incident investigation, service assurance, contract management, or management review.

### Phase 5 — Control Escalation and Approval

Communication escalation shall ensure that high-impact or time-sensitive matters are communicated promptly to the correct decision-makers and affected parties.

| Scenario | Escalation Threshold | Initial Communication Owner | Approval Required | Target Initial Notification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Life safety or emergency event | Immediate threat to health, safety, life, or building occupancy | Incident Controller / Site FM Manager | Emergency authority per incident protocol; retrospective approval acceptable if urgent | Immediate |
| Critical utility failure | Loss of power, water, HVAC, lifts, access control, fire systems, or critical building services affecting operations | FM Operations Manager | Facilities Management Director / Site Leadership depending impact | Within [X] minutes of confirmation |
| Major service disruption | FM service failure affecting multiple departments, customers, tenants, or business-critical activity | FM Operations Manager | Facilities Management Director | Within [X] hour(s) |

| High-risk planned works | Works affecting safety systems, security, access, critical infrastructure, or business continuity | Site FM Manager / Project Manager | FM Change Owner / HSE Lead / Security Lead as applicable | At least [X] business days prior |
| Supplier performance issue | Repeated SLA failure, serious nonconformity, contract breach, or unresolved corrective action | Contract Manager | Procurement Lead / Facilities Management Director | At next governance meeting or sooner if severe |
| Adverse stakeholder feedback | Significant complaint trend, executive complaint, tenant escalation, or reputational concern | FM Customer Experience Lead | FM System Owner / Communications Lead where applicable | Within [X] business days |
| Audit nonconformity | Major nonconformity or overdue corrective action affecting FMS conformity | FM Governance Lead | FM System Owner | Within [X] business days of issue |

### Phase 6 — Monitor Effectiveness and Improve

The organisation shall monitor the effectiveness of FM communications and take corrective or improvement actions where communications are late, inaccurate, inaccessible, incomplete, unauthorised, or ineffective.

Monitoring activities include:

- Review of communication-related complaints, feedback, and satisfaction survey results.
- Review of incident post-event reports for communication effectiveness.
- Review of helpdesk response data and notification timeliness.
- Review of contractor and supplier governance records.
- Internal audits against ISO 41001:2018 and related FMS processes.
- Review of communication performance during management review.
- Periodic testing of emergency and urgent notification channels.
- Verification that communication records are retained and retrievable.

Improvement actions may include:

- Updating the FM Communication Matrix.
- Revising communication templates.
- Updating distribution lists and contact data.
- Changing communication channels or frequency.
- Providing training or briefings to responsible communicators.
- Updating supplier contracts or service level requirements.
- Enhancing dashboards, portals, or notification systems.
- Introducing additional accessibility or language support.

## Review & Update Triggers

This Communication Plan shall be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur that could affect the communication requirements of the FMS.

Review and update triggers include:

- Changes to ISO 41001:2018 implementation scope, FM services, facilities, sites, building use, or service delivery model.
- Changes to organisational structure, FM roles, accountabilities, authorities, or governance forums.
- Introduction, termination, or significant change of outsourced FM service providers, contracts, service level agreements, or supplier reporting requirements.
- Changes to legal, regulatory, statutory, contractual, health, safety, environmental, security, accessibility, or business continuity communication obligations.
- Significant planned changes affecting facility users, including relocations, refurbishments, closures, new systems, changes to operating hours, or service model changes.
- Facility incidents, emergencies, disruptions, near misses, or crisis events where post-incident review identifies communication gaps.
- Internal audit findings, external audit findings, certification audit observations, nonconformities, or corrective actions relating to ISO 41001:2018.
- Management review decisions requiring changes to communication methods, frequency, content, responsibilities, or resources.
- Stakeholder complaints, satisfaction survey results, tenant feedback, or service review outcomes indicating communication is ineffective or insufficient.
- Changes to approved communication tools, helpdesk systems, intranet platforms, emergency notification systems, dashboards, or document management systems.
- Evidence that contact lists, distribution groups, escalation paths, or templates are inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated.
- New or revised risks and opportunities affecting FMS communications.
- Lessons learned from emergency drills, business continuity exercises, supplier performance reviews, or major maintenance activities.
- Changes to classification, confidentiality, privacy, or information handling requirements affecting FM communications.
- Any other material change that could affect what is communicated, when it is communicated, with whom communication takes place, how communication occurs, or who is responsible.

Updates to this Plan shall be approved by the Facilities Management Director or delegated FM System Owner. Superseded versions shall be retained in accordance with documented information control requirements.
