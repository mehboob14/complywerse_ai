## Purpose

This report documents the results of a gap analysis comparing the organisation’s current facility management operations against the requirements of ISO 41001:2018, *Facility Management System*. requires the organisation to establish, implement, maintain and continually improve a Facility Management System (FMS), including the processes needed and their interactions, in accordance with ISO 41001 requirements. This report evaluates whether current FM arrangements are sufficiently defined, controlled, integrated, monitored and improved to support an auditable ISO 41001-compliant management system.

The purpose of this report is to:

- Identify gaps between current FM operational practices and ISO 41001 expectations.
- Assess the maturity, consistency and effectiveness of current FM processes.
- Determine whether FM processes are documented, assigned, measured and integrated.
- Highlight risks to ISO 41001 implementation and certification readiness.
- Recommend corrective and improvement actions to establish a conforming FMS.

## Executive Summary

The assessment found that the organisation has several established FM operational practices, including routine maintenance, supplier coordination, service request handling and periodic management reporting. However, these practices are not yet fully configured as an integrated Facility Management System as required by ISO 41001:2018.

Current FM operations are largely activity-based and service-delivery focused, with variable levels of documentation, process ownership, performance measurement and formal continual improvement. While many operational controls exist in practice, the organisation has not yet defined a complete FMS framework showing required FM processes, their sequence, interaction, responsibilities, criteria, resources, risks, information requirements, monitoring methods and improvement mechanisms.

The principal gap is that FM activities are not consistently managed as an interconnected management system. Key processes exist, but they are not always formally documented, linked to organisational context and interested-party needs, or governed through a systematic plan-do-check-act approach.

## Methodology

### Assessment Approach

The gap analysis was performed using a structured review method aligned to ISO 41001:2018 and recognised management system auditing principles.

The methodology included:

- Review of documented FM policies, procedures, registers, plans and records.
- Interviews with FM leadership, operational staff, service owners and selected stakeholders.
- Walkthroughs of core FM processes and service delivery activities.
- Sampling of work orders, maintenance records, supplier performance records and service reports.
- Review of management reporting, KPI dashboards and issue logs.
- Assessment of process definition, ownership, controls, monitoring and improvement.
- Comparison of current arrangements against ISO 41001 requirements.

## Analysis

### Overall Maturity Assessment

The organisation’s FM operating model demonstrates practical service delivery capability but has not yet matured into a fully defined management system. Current activities are generally performed by competent personnel and service providers, but the system-level attributes expected by ISO 41001 are incomplete.

The most significant issue is the absence of a formal FMS structure connecting FM strategy, governance, operational processes, supplier activities, performance monitoring and continual improvement. Without this structure, the organisation may have difficulty demonstrating that FM operations are established, implemented, maintained and improved in accordance with ISO 41001.

### Maturity by Theme

| Theme | Current Maturity | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Establishment of the FMS | Developing | FM operations exist, but the FMS has not been formally defined as a management system with scope, governance, process architecture and control model. |
| Implementation of FMS processes | Developing | Core operational services are delivered, but implementation varies by service area, site and supplier. |
| Maintenance of the FMS | Developing | Procedures and records are maintained in some areas, but review cycles, document control and process maintenance are inconsistent. |
| Continual improvement | Initial to Developing | Improvements occur reactively but are not governed through a formal FMS improvement process. |
| Processes needed for the FMS | Developing | Many operational processes exist, but support, assurance, performance and improvement processes are incomplete. |
| Process interactions | Initial | Interactions are understood informally but are not documented or controlled through process maps or interface definitions. |
| ISO 41001 integration | Developing | Some practices support ISO 41001, but the organisation cannot yet demonstrate an integrated FMS aligned across the standard. |

### Root Cause Themes

The gaps identified appear to result from the following root cause themes:

1. **Operational focus over management system design** 
 FM teams have prioritised service continuity and operational delivery. This has created functional practices but limited formal management system architecture.

2. **Fragmented documentation environment** 
 Process information and evidence are distributed across multiple repositories and supplier systems, making it difficult to maintain control and demonstrate conformity.

3. **Limited process ownership model** 
 Responsibilities are generally understood at task level but are not consistently assigned for end-to-end FMS process performance.

4. **Variable supplier integration** 
 Outsourced services are managed through contracts and operational meetings, but supplier processes are not fully embedded within the organisation’s FMS.

5. **Reactive improvement culture** 
 Improvements are often triggered by incidents or complaints rather than systematic performance review, trend analysis or strategic improvement planning.

6. **Absence of an approved FMS process architecture** 
 Without an agreed process model, the organisation cannot clearly demonstrate process coverage, sequence, interaction and control.

### Implications for ISO 41001 Implementation

To satisfy, the organisation must be able to demonstrate more than operational activity. It must evidence an intentionally designed and controlled FMS that:

- Covers the full scope of FM activities.
- Identifies the processes required for effective FM delivery and ISO 41001 conformity.
- Defines how those processes interact.
- Assigns responsibility and authority for process operation.
- Establishes controls, criteria, resources and documented information.
- Monitors process performance and effectiveness.
- Maintains records demonstrating operation and control.
- Drives continual improvement through structured review and corrective action.

The current state provides a practical foundation for ISO 41001 implementation, but additional formalisation is required before the FMS can be considered audit-ready.
