## Purpose

This Internal Facility Management System Audit Report documents the results, findings, conclusions, and recommended actions arising from the internal audit of the organisation’s Facility Management System (FMS) conducted in accordance with ISO 41001:2018.

The purpose of this report is to provide objective evidence on whether the FMS:

- Conforms to the organisation’s own requirements for its Facility Management System;
- Conforms to the requirements of ISO 41001:2018;
- Is effectively implemented and maintained;
- Supports the intended outcomes of the FMS, including effective facility service delivery, alignment with demand organisation needs, statutory and regulatory compliance, risk-based planning, performance improvement, and user satisfaction;
- Provides suitable inputs for management review under ISO 41001:2018.

This report is intended for use by senior management, the FMS process owners, internal auditors, and other authorised stakeholders responsible for governance, assurance, corrective action, and continual improvement of the FMS.

## Executive Summary

The internal audit found that the organisation has established and implemented core elements of a Facility Management System aligned with ISO 41001:2018. The FMS demonstrates defined service delivery responsibilities, an operating model for facility services, documented procedures for key activities, routine performance monitoring, and evidence of management oversight.

However, the audit identified gaps in the maturity and consistency of the internal audit programme itself, particularly in relation to audit criteria, risk-based scheduling, independence of auditors, documentation of objective evidence, and timely closure of corrective actions. These issues are directly relevant to ISO 41001:2018, which requires the organisation to conduct internal audits at planned intervals to provide information on whether the FMS conforms to both organisational and ISO 41001 requirements and is effectively implemented and maintained.

### Overall Audit Conclusion

Based on the evidence sampled, the FMS is **partially conforming and operationally implemented**, with improvement required to strengthen assurance under and to ensure audit outputs consistently support management review, corrective action, and continual improvement.

The FMS appears capable of supporting facility management outcomes; however, the internal audit process requires formalisation and more rigorous execution to provide reliable assurance to top management and interested parties.

## Methodology

### Audit Approach

The audit was conducted using a risk-based, sample-based approach consistent with ISO 41001:2018 requirements and internal audit good practice. The audit assessed whether the FMS audit process is planned, implemented, documented, reported, and maintained in a manner that provides reliable assurance on FMS conformity and effectiveness.

Audit activities included:

- Review of the FMS audit programme and audit schedule;
- Review of audit plans, audit criteria, scope statements, and previous audit reports;
- Interviews with process owners, facility managers, service coordinators, helpdesk personnel, and contract managers;
- Sampling of documented information, including procedures, records, work orders, KPI reports, supplier performance reports, and corrective action logs;
- Review of evidence related to outsourced facility services;
- Testing of corrective action follow-up and closure evidence;
- Walkthroughs of selected FMS processes and facility service delivery controls;
- Evaluation of alignment between internal audit outputs, management review inputs, and continual improvement activities.

### Audit Criteria

The audit criteria included:

- ISO 41001:2018 requirements, with emphasis on;
- Organisation’s FMS policy, objectives, procedures, and process descriptions;
- FMS audit programme and audit planning documents;
- Service level agreements, facility service specifications, contracts, and key performance indicators;
- Applicable statutory, regulatory, and compliance obligations relevant to facility management;
- Internal requirements for document control, corrective action, risk management, and performance reporting.

### FMS-IA-2024-01 — Internal Audit Programme Not Fully Risk-Based or Complete Across FMS Scope

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Finding type | Major Nonconformity |
| ISO 41001 reference | |
| Related clauses | 4.3, 4.4, 6.1, 8.1, 8.3, 9.1 |
| Risk rating | High |

| Status | Open |

#### Requirement

ISO 41001:2018 requires the organisation to conduct internal audits at planned intervals to provide information on whether the FMS conforms to the organisation’s own requirements and to ISO 41001, and whether it is effectively implemented and maintained. The organisation must plan, establish, implement and maintain an audit programme, including frequency, methods, responsibilities, planning requirements and reporting, taking into consideration the importance of the processes concerned, changes affecting the organisation, and results of previous audits.

#### Evidence Reviewed

- FMS internal audit programme for [year];
- Internal audit schedule for [year];
- FMS scope statement;
- FMS process map;
- Previous audit reports dated [YYYY-MM-DD], [YYYY-MM-DD];
- FMS risk register;
- Management review minutes dated [YYYY-MM-DD];
- Corrective action register.

#### Finding

The internal audit programme does not consistently demonstrate that audit frequency, scope, and prioritisation are based on the importance of FMS processes, risk, change, outsourced service criticality, or previous audit results. The current audit schedule lists audit activities by calendar month, but it does not show how the programme covers the full FMS scope or why certain high-risk facility services were prioritised or deferred.

Specific gaps identified include:

- No documented mapping between the FMS scope and planned internal audits;
- Critical outsourced services such as [security / cleaning / maintenance / statutory inspection provider] not clearly included in the audit cycle;
- No evidence that previous nonconformities influenced the current audit schedule;
- No documented consideration of major changes affecting the FMS, such as [new site, contract transition, CAFM implementation, organisational restructuring];
- Some FMS processes listed in the process map have not been audited within the stated cycle.

#### Objective Evidence

| Evidence | Observation |
|---|---|
| Audit programme [year] | Contains audit dates and titles but does not include risk rationale, process importance, or previous audit result inputs |
| FMS risk register | Identifies high-risk areas including statutory maintenance and contractor control, but these are not clearly reflected in audit prioritisation |
| Previous audit report [reference] | Raised supplier performance issues, but no follow-up audit activity was scheduled |
| FMS scope statement | Includes all sites and outsourced services, but audit programme coverage is incomplete |

#### Risk / Impact

This creates a risk that significant FMS processes, outsourced services, statutory obligations, and known weak areas may not be independently assessed at planned intervals. It may also reduce the reliability of internal audit outputs used for management review and certification readiness.

#### Conclusion

The absence of a demonstrably risk-based and complete internal audit programme represents a systemic weakness in the implementation of ISO 41001:2018.

#### Required Correction / Corrective Action

- Revise the FMS internal audit programme to include the full FMS scope, processes, locations, outsourced services, and interfaces.
- Define audit frequency based on process importance, risk, changes, supplier criticality, compliance obligations, and previous audit results.
- Document the rationale for audit scheduling and prioritisation.
- Obtain approval from appropriate FMS leadership.
- Retain the revised audit programme as controlled documented information.

### Recommended Audit Report Minimum Content

Each future internal FMS audit report should include, at minimum:

- Audit objective;
- Audit criteria;
- Audit scope, including locations, functions, processes, and exclusions;
- Audit dates and audit team;
- Auditee representatives;
- Methodology and sampling statement;
- Evidence reviewed;
- Findings classified by severity;

- Positive observations;
- Conclusion on conformity and effectiveness;
- Required corrective actions;
- Responsible owners and due dates;
