# Lifecycle Management Plan

## Purpose & Objectives

The purpose of this Lifecycle Management Plan is to define how the organisation plans, coordinates, manages, and reviews the lifecycle of facility assets, services, spaces, equipment, infrastructure, and related resources from acquisition through operation, maintenance, renewal, and retirement.

This plan supports the organisation’s Facility Management System (FMS) by ensuring lifecycle decisions are coordinated with relevant interested parties in accordance with ISO 41001:2018. The plan establishes a structured approach for aligning facility asset lifecycle activities with organisational objectives, user needs, service requirements, statutory obligations, risk controls, sustainability commitments, and financial planning.

The objectives of this plan are to:

- Ensure facility assets are acquired, operated, maintained, modified, and retired in a controlled and coordinated manner.
- Align lifecycle planning with demand requirements, service levels, business continuity needs, environmental objectives, health and safety obligations, and financial constraints.
- Define coordination mechanisms between facility management, procurement, finance, operations, health and safety, sustainability, information technology, security, service providers, occupants, and other relevant interested parties.
- Ensure lifecycle-related requirements are identified, documented, approved, communicated, and reviewed.
- Support evidence-based decisions on repair, replacement, upgrade, repurposing, decommissioning, or disposal of assets.
- Improve asset availability, reliability, maintainability, usability, compliance, and total cost of ownership.
- Provide auditable records of lifecycle planning decisions, approvals, inspections, condition assessments, maintenance activities, changes, and retirements.
- Reduce risks associated with ageing infrastructure, inadequate maintenance, unplanned downtime, non-compliance, obsolete assets, inefficient resource use, and unmanaged end-of-life activities.
- Support continual improvement of the Facility Management System through performance monitoring and periodic review.

For the purpose of this plan, “facility assets” include physical assets and related services that support the built environment and organisational operations, including buildings, plant, utilities, workspaces, fixtures, fittings, critical systems, life safety systems, grounds, and contracted facility services.

## Scope

This plan applies to all facility assets, infrastructure, spaces, systems, services, and supporting resources managed under the organisation’s Facility Management System.

The scope includes the following lifecycle stages:

| Lifecycle Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Need Identification | Identification of business, operational, regulatory, user, safety, sustainability, capacity, or service needs requiring new, modified, or retired facility assets. |
| Planning & Requirements Definition | Definition of asset/service requirements, interested party expectations, risk considerations, performance criteria, budget needs, compliance obligations, and lifecycle cost assumptions. |
| Acquisition / Procurement | Procurement, leasing, design, construction, commissioning, onboarding, or contracting of assets and facility services. |
| Operation | Day-to-day operation, use, monitoring, service delivery, occupant coordination, and operational control. |
| Maintenance & Support | Preventive, corrective, predictive, statutory, and condition-based maintenance activities. |
| Change / Modification | Refurbishment, upgrade, relocation, reconfiguration, repurposing, or technical modification of assets or spaces. |
| Renewal / Replacement | Planned replacement or major renewal based on condition, performance, risk, lifecycle cost, obsolescence, or service impact. |
| Retirement / Decommissioning | Controlled removal from service, disposal, recycling, sale, transfer, demolition, data sanitisation where applicable, and record closure. |
| Review & Improvement | Evaluation of lifecycle performance, stakeholder satisfaction, service effectiveness, compliance, cost, risk, and lessons learned. |

This plan applies to:

- Owned, leased, managed, or occupied facilities.
- Buildings and structures.
- Mechanical, electrical, plumbing, heating, ventilation, air conditioning, fire protection, vertical transport, security, access control, communications, building management, and utility systems.
- Workplace furniture, fixtures, and equipment under facility management responsibility.
- Grounds, parking, external areas, and site infrastructure.
- Facility service contracts, including cleaning, maintenance, security, waste management, catering, reception, landscaping, and specialist technical services.
- Capital expenditure projects affecting facility assets.
- Operational expenditure activities supporting asset performance.
- Assets and services provided by outsourced facility management suppliers where the organisation retains accountability for requirements, performance, or compliance.

This plan does not replace detailed engineering standards, maintenance procedures, procurement procedures, project management methodologies, health and safety procedures, environmental management plans, or financial approval processes. Those documents remain applicable and shall be coordinated with this plan.

## Assumptions & Dependencies

The successful implementation of this Lifecycle Management Plan is based on the following assumptions:

- The organisation maintains an approved facility management strategy aligned with organisational objectives.
- Facility asset information is recorded in an approved asset register, computerised maintenance management system, integrated workplace management system, enterprise resource planning system, or equivalent controlled repository.
- Relevant interested parties are identifiable and available for consultation during key lifecycle decisions.
- Lifecycle decisions consider total cost of ownership, not only initial acquisition cost.
- Asset condition, criticality, service performance, statutory requirements, safety implications, environmental impact, and user requirements are considered in planning decisions.
- Funding and resource requirements for lifecycle activities are incorporated into annual budget and capital planning cycles.
- Legal, regulatory, statutory, contractual, insurance, health and safety, environmental, accessibility, and building code obligations are known or can be identified through competent subject matter experts.
- Outsourced providers are contractually required to support asset data, maintenance records, statutory inspections, reporting, and lifecycle planning.
- Business units and occupants provide timely input regarding facility needs, service impacts, operational constraints, and planned changes.
- Changes to facility assets are subject to appropriate change control, risk assessment, approval, and communication.

This plan depends on the following organisational processes, systems, and documents:

| Dependency | Required Support |
|---|---|
| Facility Management Policy | Establishes overall FM commitments, governance expectations, and service principles. |
| FM Objectives and Service Level Requirements | Define performance expectations for facility assets and services. |
| Asset Register / CMMS / IWMS | Provides controlled records of asset inventory, condition, maintenance, statutory inspections, warranties, and lifecycle status. |
| Procurement Process | Ensures acquisition decisions meet approved requirements and supplier controls. |
| Financial Planning and Budgeting Process | Supports lifecycle cost planning, capital approvals, operational expenditure planning, and replacement reserves. |
| Risk Management Process | Identifies and treats lifecycle risks, including safety, continuity, compliance, cost, and service failure risks. |
| Health, Safety and Environmental Management Processes | Ensure lifecycle activities comply with safety, environmental, waste, energy, and sustainability obligations. |
| Business Continuity Management | Identifies critical facility assets supporting essential operations and recovery requirements. |
| Project and Change Management Processes | Control modifications, refurbishment, relocation, and commissioning activities. |
| Contract and Supplier Management | Ensures outsourced facility providers meet lifecycle data, performance, compliance, and reporting obligations. |
| Records Management | Ensures retention of lifecycle evidence, approvals, inspection records, disposal records, and certificates. |
| Interested Party Engagement Process | Supports consultation, communication, and coordination required by ISO 41001. |

Key constraints and dependencies that may affect delivery include:

- Availability and accuracy of existing asset data.
- Funding approval timing and capital prioritisation.
- Access restrictions to operational facilities.
- Availability of specialist contractors, spare parts, and technical documentation.
- Regulatory approval or permit timelines.
- Operational blackout periods or restrictions on downtime.
- Lease terms, landlord approvals, or property management requirements.
- Supplier performance and contractual obligations.
- Technology integration between asset systems, finance systems, and reporting platforms.

## Approach / Phases

The organisation shall implement lifecycle management through the following coordinated phases. Each phase includes defined planning activities, engagement requirements, outputs, and decision points.

### Phase 1: Establish Lifecycle Governance and Interested Party Coordination

The organisation shall establish governance arrangements to ensure lifecycle decisions are coordinated with relevant interested parties and aligned with the Facility Management System.

Activities include:

- Confirm lifecycle management ownership and decision rights.
- Identify interested parties affected by or responsible for facility asset lifecycle decisions.
- Define communication and consultation mechanisms for lifecycle planning.
- Establish approval thresholds for acquisition, modification, renewal, and retirement decisions.
- Define asset classification, criticality, and lifecycle status categories.
- Confirm applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, safety, environmental, accessibility, and operational requirements.
- Establish escalation routes for competing priorities, funding constraints, or unresolved requirements.

Interested parties may include:

| Interested Party | Typical Lifecycle Interest |
|---|---|
| Executive Management | Strategic alignment, risk exposure, capital investment, operational resilience. |
| Facilities Management | Asset performance, maintenance, service delivery, compliance, lifecycle planning. |
| Finance | Budgeting, depreciation, capitalisation, total cost of ownership, disposal accounting. |
| Procurement | Supplier selection, contracts, warranties, service agreements, value for money. |
| Health and Safety | Safe design, statutory inspections, safe access, hazard control, legal compliance. |
| Sustainability / Environmental Management | Energy efficiency, emissions, waste, circular economy, sustainable procurement. |
| Security | Physical security systems, access control, secure decommissioning. |
| Information Technology | Smart building systems, networks, system integration, data security. |
| Business Units / Occupants | Service needs, usability, comfort, access, disruption management. |
| Legal / Compliance | Regulatory obligations, permits, lease conditions, contractual obligations. |
| External Service Providers | Maintenance delivery, statutory testing, technical advice, asset data. |
| Landlords / Property Managers | Leasehold responsibilities, building rules, approvals, shared infrastructure. |
| Regulators / Authorities | Permits, inspections, code compliance, reporting where applicable. |

Key outputs:

- Lifecycle governance model.
- Interested party register for lifecycle management.
- Lifecycle decision and approval matrix.
- Defined lifecycle communication and consultation schedule.
- Asset categorisation and criticality methodology.

### Phase 2: Asset Baseline and Requirements Definition

The organisation shall establish and maintain a reliable baseline of facility assets and define lifecycle requirements for each asset class.

Activities include:

- Validate asset inventory completeness and accuracy.
- Confirm asset ownership, location, condition, age, expected useful life, criticality, replacement value, warranty status, and maintenance requirements.
- Identify statutory inspection and certification requirements.
- Determine service level requirements, availability targets, usability requirements, safety requirements, environmental criteria, and business continuity needs.
- Assess stakeholder requirements and operational constraints.
- Identify known defects, obsolescence risks, compliance gaps, excessive operating costs, or recurring failures.
- Document asset dependencies, including utilities, controls, IT networks, specialist suppliers, and spare parts.
- Establish lifecycle risk ratings based on likelihood and consequence of asset failure or service disruption.

Asset baseline information shall include, where applicable:

| Data Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Asset ID | Unique identifier linked to asset register or CMMS. |
| Asset Description | Name and functional description of asset. |
| Location | Building, floor, room, site, zone, or system location. |
| Ownership Status | Owned, leased, tenant improvement, landlord asset, contracted service asset. |
| Commissioning Date | Date placed into operational service. |
| Expected Useful Life | Planned service life based on manufacturer, condition, and organisational criteria. |
| Condition Rating | Standardised rating based on inspection or assessment. |
| Criticality Rating | Business, safety, environmental, compliance, or service impact rating. |
| Maintenance Strategy | Preventive, predictive, corrective, statutory, run-to-failure, outsourced. |
| Compliance Requirements | Inspection, certification, permit, code, or legal requirement. |
| Operating Cost | Energy, consumables, maintenance, labour, contract, and support costs. |
| Replacement Value | Estimated replacement or renewal cost. |
| Lifecycle Status | Planned, active, under review, pending renewal, pending retirement, retired. |
| Responsible Owner | Accountable role for lifecycle oversight. |

Key outputs:

- Validated facility asset baseline.
- Asset class requirements.
- Condition and criticality profile.
- Initial lifecycle risk register.
- Identified data quality gaps and remediation actions.

### Phase 3: Lifecycle Planning and Prioritisation

The organisation shall develop lifecycle plans that support informed decision-making across acquisition, operation, maintenance, renewal, and retirement.

Activities include:

- Define lifecycle strategies by asset class.
- Develop short-, medium-, and long-term renewal forecasts.
- Prioritise lifecycle investments based on risk, compliance, performance, safety, service impact, sustainability, and cost.
- Identify maintenance optimisation opportunities.
- Evaluate options for repair, refurbish, replace, lease, outsource, upgrade, consolidate, or retire.
- Conduct lifecycle cost analysis for significant assets or investments.
- Review opportunities to improve energy efficiency, reduce emissions, minimise waste, and support sustainable use of resources.
- Align lifecycle plans with annual budgets, capital plans, operational plans, business continuity plans, and strategic facility plans.
- Confirm stakeholder acceptance of service impacts, downtime windows, decanting needs, or operational disruption.
- Define decision criteria for acquisition, continued operation, replacement, and retirement.

Lifecycle planning shall use the following decision considerations:

| Decision Area | Required Considerations |
|---|---|
| Acquisition | Need justification, stakeholder requirements, design suitability, compliance, maintainability, interoperability, supplier capability, total cost of ownership, warranty, commissioning, training, documentation, sustainability. |
| Operation | Service level alignment, safe use, capacity, access, monitoring, occupant experience, energy use, incident response, operating procedures. |
| Maintenance | Statutory duties, manufacturer guidance, risk-based maintenance, resource availability, spare parts, access, downtime impact, maintenance history. |
| Modification | Change impact, approvals, safety review, regulatory requirements, design control, commissioning, updated drawings and records, stakeholder communication. |
| Renewal | Condition, performance, reliability, obsolescence, lifecycle cost, criticality, business impact, funding availability, sustainability benefits. |
| Retirement | Decommissioning risk, disposal route, environmental controls, data/security risks where applicable, accounting treatment, stakeholder notification, record closure. |

Key outputs:

- Asset lifecycle strategy by asset class.
- Prioritised lifecycle investment plan.
- Maintenance optimisation plan.
- Renewal and replacement forecast.
- Lifecycle cost analysis for significant assets.
- Approved prioritisation criteria.

### Phase 4: Acquisition and Commissioning Control

The organisation shall ensure new assets and facility services are acquired and commissioned in accordance with approved requirements.

Activities include:

- Confirm business need and approved requirements before procurement.
- Engage relevant interested parties in specification development.
- Include lifecycle requirements in procurement specifications and contracts.
- Evaluate suppliers and solutions against total cost of ownership, maintainability, compliance, sustainability, service capability, and supportability.
- Require asset documentation, warranties, manuals, certificates, drawings, test results, training, and spare parts information.
- Conduct commissioning, acceptance testing, statutory certification, and user handover.
- Update asset register, maintenance schedules, risk assessments, service plans, and drawings before operational acceptance.
- Confirm owner and operator responsibilities.

Minimum acquisition and commissioning requirements include:

| Requirement | Expected Evidence |
|---|---|
| Approved Need / Business Case | Approved request, business case, or project mandate. |
| Interested Party Input | Meeting records, requirement sign-off, consultation notes. |
| Lifecycle Cost Assessment | Cost model or documented justification proportionate to asset significance. |
| Compliance Review | Design review, permit check, statutory requirement assessment. |
| Supplier Evaluation | Procurement evaluation records and contract award rationale. |
| Commissioning Evidence | Test certificates, commissioning reports, acceptance records. |
| Asset Data Handover | Completed asset data template, manuals, drawings, warranties. |
| Maintenance Setup | Preventive maintenance tasks loaded into CMMS or equivalent. |
| Operational Handover | Training records, handover checklist, acceptance sign-off. |

Key outputs:

- Approved acquisition records.
- Commissioned asset records.
- Updated asset register.
- Updated maintenance and compliance schedules.
- Handover and acceptance documentation.

### Phase 5: Operation, Maintenance, and Performance Management

The organisation shall operate and maintain facility assets to meet agreed requirements, manage risks, and preserve asset value throughout useful life.

Activities include:

- Implement planned maintenance, statutory inspections, corrective maintenance, and condition-based interventions.
- Monitor asset performance, service levels, failures, downtime, energy consumption, user feedback, and maintenance backlog.
- Coordinate service delivery with occupants, business units, suppliers, and other interested parties.
- Review maintenance effectiveness and adjust schedules based on asset condition, risk, manufacturer guidance, statutory obligations, and performance data.
- Manage permits, access controls, isolations, method statements, risk assessments, and safe systems of work for maintenance activities.
- Record maintenance activity, inspection results, defects, parts usage, costs, and corrective actions.
- Escalate repeated failures, safety concerns, compliance issues, and service impacts.
- Maintain contingency arrangements for critical assets.

Performance management shall include:

| Performance Area | Monitoring Method |
|---|---|
| Availability | Downtime logs, service reports, critical asset uptime data. |
| Reliability | Failure frequency, mean time between failures, repeat callouts. |
| Maintainability | Mean time to repair, spare parts availability, access constraints. |
| Compliance | Inspection completion, certificate status, overdue statutory actions. |
| Cost | Maintenance spend, lifecycle cost trends, energy cost, contractor cost. |
| Condition | Inspection ratings, condition survey results, defect trends. |
| User Satisfaction | Complaints, surveys, service feedback, helpdesk trends. |
| Sustainability | Energy use, water use, waste, emissions, efficiency improvements. |

Key outputs:

- Maintenance records.
- Compliance inspection records.
- Asset performance reports.
- Defect and corrective action logs.
- Updated lifecycle risk register.
- Maintenance improvement actions.

### Phase 6: Change, Renewal, and Replacement

The organisation shall control changes, renewals, and replacements to facility assets to prevent unintended impacts and ensure continued conformity with requirements.

Activities include:

- Identify assets requiring renewal or replacement through condition assessment, performance data, risk review, compliance findings, or stakeholder requirements.
- Develop renewal business cases based on risk, service impact, lifecycle cost, sustainability, and strategic alignment.
- Review design, technical, safety, environmental, accessibility, security, IT, and operational implications.
- Coordinate planned works with affected interested parties.
- Approve shutdowns, access restrictions, relocations, temporary services, or business continuity arrangements.
- Manage changes through approved project or change control processes.
- Commission modified or replaced assets before return to service.
- Update drawings, asset data, maintenance schedules, operating procedures, risk assessments, and service documentation.

Change control shall address:

| Change Control Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Change Request | Document purpose, scope, affected assets, and reason for change. |
| Impact Assessment | Assess safety, compliance, service, financial, environmental, security, IT, accessibility, and user impacts. |
| Interested Party Consultation | Consult affected parties before approval and implementation. |
| Approval | Obtain authority based on financial, risk, and operational thresholds. |
| Implementation Plan | Define schedule, responsibilities, controls, communication, and contingency arrangements. |
| Testing and Commissioning | Verify asset performance and compliance before acceptance. |
| Record Update | Update asset register, drawings, maintenance plans, certificates, and procedures. |
| Post-Implementation Review | Confirm intended benefits and capture lessons learned. |

Key outputs:

- Approved renewal and replacement projects.
- Change impact assessments.
- Updated asset records.
- Commissioning and acceptance records.
- Post-implementation review outcomes.

### Phase 7: Retirement, Decommissioning, and Disposal

The organisation shall manage retirement and disposal of facility assets in a controlled manner to protect safety, compliance, financial value, operational continuity, environmental performance, and records integrity.

Activities include:

- Identify assets for retirement based on condition, obsolescence, performance, risk, service changes, redundancy, lease exit, or replacement.
- Confirm that retirement will not adversely affect service delivery, safety, compliance, security, resilience, or other dependent assets.
- Develop decommissioning plans for significant or high-risk assets.
- Obtain approvals from asset owner, finance, facilities, health and safety, environmental management, procurement, and other relevant interested parties.
- Safely isolate, dismantle, remove, dispose, recycle, sell, transfer, or return assets.
- Manage hazardous materials, regulated waste, permits, contractor controls, and environmental obligations.
- Remove or sanitise data-bearing components where facility systems include digital controls, access systems, CCTV, storage media, or network-connected devices.
- Update asset registers, maintenance plans, financial records, drawings, warranties, contracts, and compliance schedules.
- Retain disposal certificates, waste transfer notes, sale records, transfer records, or destruction certificates as applicable.
- Review lessons learned and update lifecycle assumptions.

Minimum retirement requirements include:

| Requirement | Expected Evidence |
|---|---|
| Retirement Justification | Condition report, replacement decision, redundancy confirmation, or risk assessment. |
| Impact Assessment | Service, safety, compliance, security, environmental, financial, and stakeholder impact review. |
| Approval | Retirement approval record from authorised roles. |
| Decommissioning Plan | Method, schedule, isolation requirements, contractor controls, waste handling, contingency actions. |
| Disposal Route | Recycling, resale, transfer, return, regulated disposal, demolition, or waste contractor documentation. |
| Environmental Controls | Waste notes, hazardous materials documentation, environmental permits where required. |
| Security / Data Controls | Sanitisation certificate or access control removal confirmation where applicable. |
| Record Closure | Asset register update, finance update, maintenance schedule closure, drawing update. |

Key outputs:

- Approved retirement records.
- Decommissioning and disposal evidence.
- Updated asset and financial records.
- Environmental and security disposal evidence.
- Lessons learned.

### Phase 8: Review, Reporting, and Continual Improvement

The organisation shall review lifecycle management performance and improve lifecycle planning based on evidence, interested party feedback, risk trends, audit findings, and service outcomes.

Activities include:

- Review lifecycle performance against approved success metrics.
- Report significant asset risks, lifecycle funding gaps, compliance issues, and service impacts to management.
- Conduct periodic lifecycle planning workshops with relevant interested parties.
- Review asset data quality and completeness.
- Evaluate lifecycle plan effectiveness following major acquisitions, renewals, failures, or retirements.
- Identify opportunities for standardisation, sustainability improvement, maintenance optimisation, supplier improvement, and cost reduction.
- Incorporate lessons learned into planning criteria, procurement specifications, maintenance strategies, and decision models.
- Provide inputs to FMS management review.

Key outputs:

- Lifecycle performance dashboard.
- Lifecycle risk and investment report.
- Improvement action register.
- Updated lifecycle plan.
- Inputs to ISO 41001 management review.

## Review & Update Triggers

This plan shall be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur that may affect facility asset lifecycle requirements, interested party needs, operational performance, legal obligations, risk exposure, or Facility Management System conformity.

Mandatory review triggers include:

- Changes to organisational strategy, facility management objectives, service level requirements, or operating model.
- Significant acquisition, disposal, lease, relocation, refurbishment, consolidation, or expansion of facilities.
- Introduction of new asset classes, technologies, smart building systems, or critical facility services.
- Major asset failure, incident, near miss, compliance breach, safety event, environmental event, or business disruption.
- Significant changes in legal, regulatory, statutory, contractual, insurance, building code, accessibility, health and safety, or environmental requirements.
- Material changes to stakeholder requirements, occupancy levels, usage patterns, business processes, or service demand.
- Changes in outsourced facility management providers, maintenance contractors, landlords, or property management arrangements.
- Budget constraints or funding changes that affect lifecycle risk treatment, maintenance strategy, renewal timing, or compliance obligations.
- Internal audit, external audit, certification audit, management review, or assurance findings requiring lifecycle management changes.
- Performance metrics indicating persistent underperformance, increasing failures, growing maintenance backlog, rising lifecycle risk, or poor user satisfaction.
- Identification of significant asset data quality issues.
- Sustainability commitments requiring changes to asset operation, renewal, procurement, or disposal strategies.
- Business continuity or resilience reviews identifying facility asset dependencies or weaknesses.
- Lessons learned from acquisition, commissioning, modification, renewal, retirement, or decommissioning activities.
- Changes to ISO 41001 interpretation, organisational management system requirements, or applicable best practice.

Review activities shall include:

| Review Activity | Description | Responsible Role |
|---|---|---|
| Plan Effectiveness Review | Assess whether lifecycle planning activities are being implemented and achieving intended outcomes. | Head of Facilities Management |
| Interested Party Review | Confirm whether lifecycle coordination mechanisms remain effective and interested party needs are understood. | FM Governance Lead |
| Asset Data Review | Assess completeness, accuracy, and timeliness of asset lifecycle information. | Asset Manager |
| Risk Review | Review lifecycle risk register, risk treatments, and escalation items. | FM Risk Coordinator |
| Performance Review | Evaluate success metrics, trends, exceptions, and corrective actions. | FM Performance Analyst |
| Compliance Review | Confirm statutory inspections, certifications, and legal requirements are current. | Health and Safety Manager |
| Budget Review | Confirm lifecycle plans remain aligned to approved funding and capital planning. | Finance Business Partner |
| Lessons Learned Review | Incorporate improvements from lifecycle activities, incidents, projects, audits, and stakeholder feedback. | Facilities Operations Manager |
| Document Update | Revise this plan, obtain approval, communicate updates, and retain version history. | Document Owner |

Updates to this plan shall be approved by the Head of Facilities Management or delegated authority. Material changes affecting governance, approval thresholds, lifecycle decision criteria, or resource commitments shall be escalated to executive management for approval.
