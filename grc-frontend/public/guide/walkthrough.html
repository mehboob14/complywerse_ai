IT Assets

The register of everything you own. Every score in the module ultimately hangs off a row in this list.

The register. Click any row to open its detail page.

1
Asset name
Identity. An "@ address" suffix means this asset was promoted from detected software — an application running on a host, given its own record.From: manual entry, CSV import, agent discovery, or software promotion
2
Criticality
The most consequential field in the module. It feeds vulnerability priority (10% of it), the CIA dimension of posture, and one of the six remediation triggers.From: derived from C/I/A ratings, or a formal criticality assessment
3
Risk score
The composite posture score, 0–100, higher is worse. Identical to the number on the Risk Posture page — one asset, one risk number.From: the risk posture engine, five weighted dimensions
4
Findings
Count of open vulnerabilities. Remediated, verified, closed, resolved and accepted findings are excluded — closing one drops this number.From: linked vulnerabilities, filtered by status
Why this page comes first

You cannot rank risk on things you have not listed. Every other screen in this module is a view through an asset — a vulnerability with no asset is an abstract fact about software, not a risk to your business.

Asset detail

Twelve tabs, grouped into five questions. Everything known about one machine.

Overview tab shown. The five bracketed groups are how the twelve tabs are meant to be read.

1
Overview tab — identity and the five headline numbers
Ownership, network, hardware, procurement. It also absorbed the old Discovery and Assignments tabs, whose fields already lived here.From: the asset record plus the posture engine for the score
2
Blast radius = 0
Unmapped, not contained. No relationship has been declared and nothing shares its address. Declare dependencies on the Relationships tab and this fills in.From: declared relationships + assets sharing a routable IP (loopback excluded)
3
Control coverage = 0%
Zero of a target of twelve controls linked. This same percentage appears on Risk & Controls and drives 15% of the posture score — one formula, three places.From: control links ÷ 12
Reading empty numbers correctly

Blast radius 0 and coverage 0% both mean nobody has recorded this yet. They are not evidence of isolation or of a control gap being closed. Absence of data and a good result look identical if you only read the number.

Asset posture detail

The score, broken into its five dimensions — and the confidence behind it.

The same asset shown earlier — 34.9/100, with three dimensions excluded.

1
The percentage is the effective share
Vulnerabilities is weighted 30% by default — but with three dimensions excluded it carries 67% of this asset's score. Showing the raw 30% beside 18.3 pts made the legend look like broken arithmetic.From: weight ÷ sum of known weights

"no data, excluded"
Zero points here means unknown, not "no gap". Without that label, "Control gap → 0 pts" reads as good news when it means nothing has been measured.From: the known flag on each dimension

The share of scoring weight actually backed by evidence. Read it with the score, never alone. A low score at 45% quality is a much weaker claim than the same score at 95%.From: sum of known weights
The single most common misreading

A new asset with nothing recorded shows a low score and a reassuring green band, because it is judged on one or two dimensions. That is "we don't know", not "it's fine."

Vulnerabilities

Every finding across the estate, ranked. This is the work queue.

ColumnMeansComes from
SeverityHow bad the flaw is in generalCVSS, from NVD
PriorityHow urgent it is for you, 0–100Our 7-signal engine
Statusopen → in progress → remediated → verifiedYour workflow
Due dateDeadline by severityYour SLA policy
KEV / exploit chipsThreat flags at a glanceCISA, GitHub
Sort by priority, not severity

A CVSS 9.8 with no published exploit, on an internal box, behind a patch you already applied, is not your most urgent problem. Severity describes the flaw; priority describes your exposure to it.

What "EPSS" means when you see it here

EPSS ships two numbers from FIRST: the probability of exploitation in the next 30 days (0.5% means 0.5%), and the percentile (37th means it ranks above 37% of all CVEs). They are not interchangeable — reading a percentile as a probability overstates risk by roughly seventy times.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Risk & Controls

Risk & Controls

The asset’s risk score broken into its parts, the controls covering it, and the risks linked to it.

What is on this tab

BlockShowsComes from
Residual riskComposite score + a bar per dimensionThe posture engine — same endpoint as the Risk Posture page
CIA cardConfidentiality / Integrity / Availability, each 1–5Set by a human, or derived from criticality
CIS cardBenchmark pass-rate summaryCompliance scan runs
Linked controlsWhich controls cover this asset, and coverage %Control links ÷ target of 12
Mapping recommendationsControls the system suggests linkingAsset profile signals — OS, vendor, business function
Linked risksRisk-register entries naming this assetRisk-asset links

CIA — what it means

These describe what happens if this asset is compromised, not how likely that is. 1 = public / harmless / can be down indefinitely. 5 = regulated / legally harmful / downtime stops the business.

Set vs assumed

If nobody sets them, values are derived from criticality and the dimension is marked not known — excluded from the score rather than counted as evidence. That lowers data quality, which is the honest outcome.

Coverage, and why 12

Coverage is linked controls ÷ 12. There is no universal right number — twelve is a house convention that makes assets comparable. What matters is that every screen uses the same one: an asset reading 8% here must read 8% on Overview and inside the score.

Why suggestions stay suggestions

Mapping controls by hand across a large estate is the most-skipped task in GRC. Recommendations turn authoring into reviewing. They are not applied automatically because a control mapping is an assertion you may have to defend to an auditor — a human accepts it.

Why “accepted forever” is named

Nobody consciously decides to accept a risk permanently — it is what you get when the review-date field is left blank. Six months later it is invisible and unreviewed. Naming it is the difference between a decision and an oversight.

The findings list

Severity, priority, status, due date. The Open Findings figure on Overview counts only open statuses — closing a finding drops it.

This tab absorbed the old Alerts tab

Alerts were built entirely from the same vulnerability join this list uses, plus one staleness check. It was a severity ranking of this list, not a second dataset — so it now sits on top of the list it ranks.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Software

Software

What is installed, and which of it can be matched to CVEs.

Installed software

Package inventory from discovery — name, version, publisher. Descriptive on its own.

Software identifiers (CPE / PURL)

A CPE is the standard machine-readable product name (cpe:2.3:a:apache:log4j:2.14.1). It is how NVD describes what a CVE affects. Without one, software cannot be automatically matched to vulnerabilities.

Business translation

“We have Log4j 2.14.1” and “we are affected by Log4Shell” are only connected if something speaks both languages. CPE is that language. An estate with no identifiers can only find vulnerabilities by scanning — never by matching a published advisory against what it already knows.

Promote to asset

Turns a detected application into its own asset record, a child of the host. Do it when the application deserves its own owner, criticality and control mappings (a database engine). Leave it as software when it does not (a PDF reader).

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Relationships

Relationships

Declared dependencies between assets — your CMDB graph.

TypeMeaning
depends_onThis asset needs the other to function
hosts / runs_onPhysical or virtual containment
connects_toNetwork reachability
backs_up / replicates_toData flows to the other asset
member_ofPart of a cluster or group

Relationships drive blast radius — what an attacker reaches next — and change the Lateral Movement stage of the exploit assessment.

Empty means unmapped, not safe

No declared relationships shows an empty blast radius. That is an absence of data, not evidence of isolation. Loopback addresses (127.0.0.1) are deliberately excluded from co-location grouping — every locally-discovered asset carries them, so grouping on them merges unrelated machines.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Lifecycle

Lifecycle

Where the asset sits in its life, and when it stops being supported.

Requested → procured → deployed → active → maintenance → retired → disposed, plus warranty and end-of-life dates.

Why security cares about a procurement field

An asset past end-of-life stops receiving vendor patches. At that point “apply the vendor patch” is no longer an available remediation, and every finding on it becomes a compensating-control or replacement decision.

Known gap

State changes update the asset in place. There is no transition table, so there is no record of who changed a lifecycle state or when.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Compliance

Compliance — CIS benchmark

Hardening checks. A vulnerability is a defect someone else shipped; a benchmark failure is a configuration you chose.

CIS publishes hardening baselines per platform — hundreds of checks like “password minimum length ≥ 14”. Each passes, fails, errors, or was never run.

pass rate        = passed ÷ total applicable rules
scanned gap      = failed ÷ (passed + failed)
coverage penalty = never-scanned ÷ total
CIS gap          = 0.8 × scanned gap + 0.2 × coverage penalty

Two things are measured at once on purpose: how much failed, and how much was never checked. Ten rules passed out of ten scanned, with 200 never run, is not a hardened asset.

SituationResult
No applicable rules, or none ever runNot known — excluded from the score
Runs exist but every one erroredGap 1.0 (worst) — a broken scan is maximum uncertainty, not a clean bill of health
Mixed pass / failFormula above

Co-located blending

Assets sharing a real host address blend — 60% the host’s own score, 40% a criticality-weighted average of its applications. A hardened host running an unpatched database is not a hardened environment. Borrowed scores are labelled as borrowed.

This tab also holds scan-run history, which absorbed the old Activity tab — that called the same endpoint with a smaller limit.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Trajectory

Trajectory

Asset → vulnerability → business risk, drawn as a chain.

The same records as the Vulnerabilities and Risk & Controls tabs, presented as a graph rather than a list. Click a node to trace its sub-chain.

Who this is for

Explaining a path to a non-technical audience. “This server has a flaw, that flaw threatens customer data, that is risk R-014 on the register” is a sentence a board understands; a table of CVEs is not.

Honest note

This is a fifth view of data already shown elsewhere. It earns its place if it is used in review meetings — otherwise it is duplication.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Criticality

Criticality Assessments

The formal, reviewed version of how much this asset matters.

  • ISCA — Information System Criticality Assessment. Rates the system as a whole.
  • IACA — Information Asset Criticality Assessment. Rates the data it holds.

Each produces a score, a band and a status (draft, submitted, approved). Approved assessments are what an auditor asks to see.

Why a workflow exists alongside a simple field

The CIA sliders let an engineer record a working judgement in ten seconds. A regulator wants something else: a documented assessment with a named approver and a date. Both are legitimate; they serve different audiences.

Known gap

The formal assessment and the criticality derived from CIA do not reconcile. The scoring engine uses the derived value. Where they disagree, nothing flags it.

IT Assets → Asset 06 → Attachments · Notes · History

The record

Evidence, human context, and the audit trail.

Attachments

Evidence documents — architecture diagrams, approvals, scan reports. In an audit, an assertion without an artefact behind it is just a claim.

Notes

Free-text, attributed, timestamped. For context that does not fit a field: “scheduled for decommission Q3, do not remediate”.

History

Who changed which field, when. Populated from the moment journalling was enabled; assets edited before then show nothing.

Notes and History are deliberately separate

History is what the system observed. Notes are what a person chose to record. Merging them loses the distinction between fact and reasoning.

The score breakdown

Seven signals, each showing the points contributed and the evidence behind it. CVSS is only 20% — 60% of the weight sits on exploitability and context, because two findings with identical CVSS can differ enormously in real risk.

Unknown is shown as unknown

With no CVSS vector stored, attack vector reads unknown and the breakdown says the value was assumed. It does not render a confident tick for data that does not exist.

Linked controls

Which of your controls this finding undermines — auto-mapped from its CWE (weakness type) or linked by hand. The bridge between “a server is unpatched” and “control A.12.6.1 is not effective”.

Vulnerabilities → VULN-37 → Remediation

Remediation

What are we doing about it? Three groups, because there are only three possible answers.

Group 1 — The fix

Why this needs action now: the red flags that justified acting, each naming its evidence. If none fire, the panel says so and the finding waits for the normal patch cycle.

The plan: title, fix type, a copyable artefact of the actual steps, and a rationale — generated from the CVE, asset type, OS family and exposure. A draft shows immediately without being saved; adopting it is the moment of intent, because a saved plan carries an approval trail.

StageRecordsGate
RecommendedPlan text + why it was warranted—
ApprovedWho authorised it, change windowRequires an owner
AppliedExecution log of the stepsAuto-apply refused on critical assets
VerifiedAttestation with evidenceRequires evidence text
Two claims this deliberately does not make

“Applied” does not patch anything. Execution runs through a simulated executor that records steps without touching a host.

“Verified” does not re-scan. It records that a named person attested the fix is in place, and what they offered as grounds. Evidence is mandatory — a confirmation with nothing behind it looks like proof in an audit export.

Group 2 — Who owns it

Department assignment with its own SLA override and escalation path. Distinct from the individual assignee in the sidebar: one routes to a person, one to a queue. Either satisfies the approval gate.

Group 3 — If we are not fixing it yet

Mitigations are tracked tasks with owner, priority, status and target date — the work that reduces exposure without removing the flaw. Suggest compensating controls proposes candidates; it is forbidden from proposing the patch or asserting patch availability. Exception workflow is the formal “we are deliberately not fixing this”, with a review date that makes it expire.

Remediate vs mitigate vs accept

Remediation — the flaw is gone. Mitigation — still there, you put a guard on it. Acceptance — still there, and you have decided to live with it, on the record, with a date. Only three answers, which is why the tab has three groups.

Vulnerabilities → VULN-37 → Exploit Test

Exploit Test

Could this actually be reached and used — and how far would it spread?

Nothing is executed

This reads stored evidence only. No scanner, exploit or payload runs against any host. Every verdict is a derivation from data already held.

Group 1 — Can it be reached?

The MITRE ATT&CK kill chain: six stages, each marked reached, possible or blocked. Clicking a stage explains why it is in that state and what would stop it.

StageTechniqueQuestion
ReconnaissanceT1595Can an attacker find this asset?
Initial accessT1190Can they get in through this flaw?
ExecutionT1203Can they run code?
Privilege escalationT1068Can they become admin?
Lateral movementT1210Can they reach other assets?
ImpactT1005What can they take or break?

The evidence table gives one row per fact: what we know, and what would block it. Five checks — internet-facing, network attack vector, known-exploited, patch unapplied, segmentation.

These blockers are heuristics

They are derived from the same evidence, inverted. They are not linked to your ISO or NIST control library, so a “blocked” step is not yet usable as control evidence.

Group 2 — How far would it spread?

Blast radius: assets reachable next, each labelled by how we know — a declared relationship (a stated fact) or a shared address (our inference). Vulnerability chain: prerequisite findings that must also exist, and findings that depend on this one.

Why prerequisites change urgency

A privilege-escalation flaw is far more urgent when a remote-code-execution flaw lets an attacker reach that server in the first place. Fixing the RCE makes the escalation academic. Chains let you fix one thing and close several.

Group 3 — Prove it

The retest log: date, tester, result (pass / fail / partial), findings, evidence. Everything above is derivation; this is the only part that is proof. Until a retest exists, remediation is claimed but not demonstrated.

Vulnerabilities → VULN-37 → History & Notes

History & Notes

What happened, and what people said about it.

History

Every status change, assignment, plan adoption, approval, application and attestation — with actor and timestamp.

Why this is the tab an auditor opens first

An auditor rarely disputes that you fixed something. They ask who decided, when, and on what basis. A product that changes state without recording those three things cannot answer, however good its workflow looks.

Notes

Free-text, attributed, timestamped. For reasoning that must outlive a status field: why an exception was granted, what the vendor said, why this is a false positive.
